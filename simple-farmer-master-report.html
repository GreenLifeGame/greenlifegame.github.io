01

Executive Summary

Simple Farmer is a Solana-themed play-to-earn crop-farming web game — the second title on the GreenLifeGame (GLG) platform, and deliberately the simpler/easier one. Where GREEN LIFE is a deep cannabis-farming economy with Mythical breeding, a 3D world, PvP games, tournaments, weapons/defend, clans and a 22,000-line codebase, Simple Farmer strips the same core shape (buy a farm, plant cards, earn a token, breed, trade, refer friends) down to fewer systems and a flatter, more approachable economy — fruits and vegetables instead of strains, a 5-tier rarity ladder with no Mythical ceiling, and an overhead SVG farm map instead of a walkable 3D room.

Current build status: the game is a fully simulated client-side prototype. The landing screen states this outright — "The wallet is simulated — nothing connects to Solana and no signature in this build is real." Every balance, purchase, harvest, breed and claim happens as in-memory React state; nothing persists past a page refresh, and no real money moves anywhere in this codebase today.

Core game loop

Connect wallet (simulated)→ Invite code (roulette matches you if you have none)→ $25 buy-in, SOL/USDC/USDT→ Farm mints + $5 $GREEN bonus + 1 Starter Pack auto-opens→ Plant & earn every second→ Rip packs / crossbreed / boost→ Level the farm / unlock plots→ Trade / refer / play the lottery / repeat

Navigation is a bottom bar of six tabs plus a conditional seventh: Farm · Garden · Breed · Market · Stuff · World — and, when the (currently wide-open) admin flag is set, a seventh 🛠 Admin tab. Market is its own full tab here — unlike GREEN LIFE, where Market is a block inside World; Simple Farmer's World screen instead holds the shared emissions pool, a simulated leaderboard, a pool-life decay projection, and the honest simulated-systems disclosure list (§19, §25).

What makes it "the simple one" — the deliberate simplifications vs. GREEN LIFE

  • No Mythical tier. Legendary is the ceiling, full stop — five rarities (Common → Legendary), not six.
  • One card per pack, always — a Seed/Mystery/Boost/Starter/PRIMO pull is exactly one card, never a multi-card bundle, so a tier's stated odds are literally the odds of that one pull.
  • No watering mechanic. Plants grow purely on elapsed time; there is no Water All button and nothing dehydrates.
  • No 3D room. The farm is a single inline SVG overhead map — deliberately not Three.js, no textures, no draw-call budget to manage (see §22).
  • No PvP games, no tournaments, no weapons/Defend, no clans, no emote system. None of these systems exist in Simple Farmer's current build — a control for a system that isn't there would be worse than no control.
  • A category-gated breeding rule with a real category exclusivity (Veggie×Veggie or Fruit×Fruit only, never across) that GREEN LIFE's breeding does not have.
  • A genuinely different in-tier pack-odds mechanic — value-inverse weighting (cheaper card = more likely) rather than GREEN LIFE's flat 60/30/10 split (see §07).

Relationship to GREEN LIFE & the GLG Platform

GreenLifeGame (GLG) is the shared platform brand across every GLG title. Simple Farmer is one of at least two confirmed real games under it (its sister is GREEN LIFE, the cannabis-farming game); the platform's own supply plan reserves pools for a total of three games at minimum.

The one and only thing shared between games GLG games share ONLY the $GREEN token and its liquidity pool — never code, never a repo, never mechanics. Simple Farmer is its own file set (farmer-*.jsx/farmer-*.js), its own React entry point (farmer-main.jsx mounting into farmer.html), and imports nothing from GREEN LIFE's constants.js/green-game.jsx. The reverse is also true. This is stated as a deliberate isolation choice — reusing GREEN LIFE's UI file, for example, would pull in strain art and weapon tables and "tie the two games together at the code level for no benefit."

Why the pool sizing matters mechanically

"the goal is for all 3 games to have similar earning power, the more advanced games will earn a little more bc it has a higher emission pool and take more player time to play." — Cody, 2026-08-18

All three games run the same 0.069%/day decay, so what separates a player's earning power between games is purely the size of that game's own pool. A player holding the same share of Simple Farmer's own network therefore earns at roughly a 1 : 1.33 : 1.67 ratio versus the same share in the Weed and Dino games respectively — close enough that no game feels pointless, tilted toward the heavier games that ask more of a player's time. The consequence worth holding onto: earning power is set by pool size, not by Simple Farmer's own crop numbers — making crops more powerful across the board does not increase real payout, because emissions split by share of network power, and raising everyone's power raises the denominator too. The only levers that move real earnings here are the pool allocation and the decay rate.

Platform-shared presentation

  • Both games open on an identical GLG platform splash (GlgIntro in Simple Farmer, its own separate component from GREEN LIFE's equivalent — components are never shared, only art and copy) — same wordmark, same copy: "FIXED SUPPLY · ZERO INFLATION · P2E ON SOLANA" / "420B · MINTED ONCE · NEVER AGAIN" / "tap to enter." Cody's own framing: "this should be the exact intro on all games, the words should match also" — these are platform-wide $GREEN facts, not game-specific ones, so they read identically everywhere.
  • Only the game's own icon art (/glg-brand/glg-icon.png) is shared between the two splashes — same category as sharing the token itself.
  • Simple Farmer's own brand accent color, #52e300, is the exact same green GREEN LIFE uses as its own accent — pulled from the same GLG-family logo palette.

Deliberately separate, and why

  • Separate referral graphs. Each game runs its own 3-level referral chain, its own bootstrap wallets, its own roulette pool. A friend referred in one game is not automatically connected in the other.
  • Separate asset folders. Boost card art lives at public/farmer-boosts/, deliberately not GREEN LIFE's public/boosts/ — the two folders sharing a similar name nearly caused Simple Farmer's renders to save into GREEN LIFE's own asset directory during production; the source explicitly warns never to merge them back.
  • Separate admin consoles, separate wallets registry, separate SIM_FLAGS list. Nothing about Simple Farmer's Admin Console reads or writes GREEN LIFE's state.
  • Deliberately fewer systems. Simple Farmer is positioned as the platform's Easy difficulty tier; GREEN LIFE carries the deep systems (PvP, tournaments, weapons/Defend, clans, a 3D world). A system missing from Simple Farmer is very often not an oversight — it is the tier boundary doing its job.
Gap in the research The research passes available for this report cover only Simple Farmer's own source files in depth. Confirmation of the exact three-game platform split (Farm 150B / Weed 200B / Dino 250B out of 690B) comes from comments inside farmer-constants.js itself describing the shared plan — no GREEN LIFE or "Dino Island" source file was read as part of producing this document, so anything about those other games beyond what Simple Farmer's own comments state should be treated as Simple Farmer's own understanding of the plan, not independently verified against those games' code.

The "How to Play" modal — 7 steps, every number read live

Every figure in this modal is computed from the actual live constants at render time (never hand-typed) specifically so it can never drift from what the game really charges — though the source itself documents that this exact promise was still caught failing once (a hardcoded currency label slipped through the 2026-08-24 audit; see §26).

1 · Connect & enter
Connect a wallet, enter an invite code, pay the $25.00 entry to mint your Level 1 farm. It opens itself right away.
2 · Plant & earn every second
70% of your farmed $GREEN streams the instant it's earned; the other 30% holds in a pot that only pays out at harvest.
3 · Rip packs
Seed $1.50 · Mystery $1.00 · Boost $0.50 (all $GREEN today) — at the live price and currency, whatever they're currently set to. No daily limit on any of them.
4 · Upgrade
Spend $GREEN to level your farm (Lv2–5, $10.00 flat each): more garden plots, higher pack limits. Each level has its own build-time cooldown.
5 · Emission decay
Every 24 hours the network emits 0.069% of whatever's left in the rewards pool — a slow, sustainable curve instead of a spike.
6 · Burn
Every $GREEN spent in-game is burned forever — never returned to the pool.
7 · Refer & earn
3% of your direct referrals' earnings and pack buys, 1% from their referrals, 0.01% one level deeper. Three levels — that's the cap, always.

Stage 2 — "checking" (the existing-farm lookup)

Roughly a 900ms artificial delay standing in for an on-chain lookup. No buttons on this screen on purpose — nothing has been decided or charged yet. Copy: "Checking your farm… Looking up whether {wallet} already owns one. Connecting your wallet is the login — if you already farm here you go straight in. No code, no second buy-in."

Why there is no "I already have an account" button Connecting the wallet IS the login. A farm lives at an address derived from the wallet itself, so the game just looks: if this wallet owns one, the player is dropped straight back into it, never asked for an invite code, never asked to buy in again. A separate button for "I'm returning" can be tapped wrongly by a new player, and tapping it wrongly could walk an existing owner toward paying the $25 entry a second time.

findExistingFarm() is an explicit stub in this build — it always returns null (gated by a DEMO_EXISTING_FARM testing flag, currently false), so every connect in this build onboards as brand new.

Stage 3 — "invite" (the code screen)

Split into its own screen on 2026-08-24 specifically to mirror GREEN LIFE's own two-screen invite/entry split "screenshot-for-screenshot." This screen's only job is collecting a code — no price, no wallet, no "what you get" belongs here.

  • Entry is invite-only, no skip. Continue is hard-gated on the code being at least 4 characters — a real generated code is 4 letters + 3 digits = 7 characters, but the minimum-to-continue is looser to allow shorter/longer aliases in the future.
  • Code alphabet: ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ — I and O deliberately excluded so a code read off a screen can never be confused with 1 and 0.
  • 🎲 Referral roulette matches a code-less player to a real seeded ancestor from the bootstrap chain (SFROOT3/SFROOT4/SFROOT5 only — SFROOT1/SFROOT2 are structurally excluded), not a throwaway random string — every code it can hand out has real, known ancestors 3 levels up, so a brand-new player's referral cut resolves correctly from their very first action.
  • Optional claim code field — if a valid claim code is typed here, it waives the entry fee entirely on the next screen (validated live via checkClaimCode() before the player even continues).

Render order, deliberately matching GREEN LIFE's dashboard layout per Cody's own instruction ("make the emission box and stat info match weed game by being at the top"):

Current Estimate→ Stat grid→ Farm Level→ Farm Scene (the overhead map)→ Daily block→ Lottery (4-pool grid)→ Pack carousel (incl. PRIMO)

Every price on this screen is read from the SHOP registry, never from the raw pack table. The admin-editable SHOP_CONFIG is the one dial that decides what's charged; PACKS/PRIMO are only read here for name/emoji/card-count — the things that aren't price.

Current Estimate block

  • The only $GREEN figure in the entire game shown as exact digits, never abbreviated — "watching the exact digits tick is the point of it."
  • Shows the unclaimed balance (the 70% instant-stream half only), its USD equivalent, the player's live share of network earning power, and a mini progress bar for the emissions pool balance vs. its original size.

The multiplier math

Locked rule A garden's multiplier scales a planted card's farm power UP by that factor AND divides its harvest cycle DOWN by the same factor. Net effect: a single harvest pays exactly the same total whichever tier it's in — rate doubled while time halved cancels out. The real benefit of a higher tier is THROUGHPUT: over a fixed stretch of real time you finish more cycles, so total earnings scale linearly with the multiplier, not exponentially.

Cody's own words: "if a veggie is 7days 1x on 2x it should be 3.5days and earn the same amount it would from 7 days but in 3.5 days."

Formulas: cycleMs(cropKey, mult) = (CROPS[cropKey].days / mult) × growDayMs(); plantPower(cropKey, mult, boostPct) = CROPS[cropKey].power × mult × (1 + boostPct/100).

Slot ownership & unlocking

  • The 1X Garden auto-unlocks the moment the farm level allows it — no purchase, no button. It is the only tier with solPerSlot === 0, so owned just tracks the level-derived cap directly.
  • Every other tier requires an explicit SOL purchase per plot, one at a time, strictly in order — the game only ever offers the next unbought plot for sale; tapping ahead in the queue is blocked with "take plot N first."
  • Purchases route through the real SOL/USDC/USDT pay-with picker and a wallet-sign screen (fixed in the 2026-08-24 audit — it used to charge SOL directly with no confirmation).
  • The Garden screen renders every plot the tier could ever reach at farm Level 5, not just what's unlocked today — locked cells show what farm level unlocks them and at what price, so a player can see what leveling up actually buys.

Planting — a staged batch that signs once

Cody, 2026-08-24 "when planting cards in the game make sure there is a sign to complete the task at the end they can do multiple in and out but they have to sign to finalize it. that is a final decision. weed game has this but simple farmer needs it."

A card tapped into a plot becomes PENDING — not a real plant, not earning yet — until one signature commits the whole batch. Tapping a pending card again pulls it back out for free, no signature either way; only the final "🔐 Confirm & Sign" commits. The staging bar shows the GLOBAL staged count across every garden tier at once, not just the one currently on screen, since one signature commits all of them together.

Boosts on a growing plant

One active boost per plant, no stacking, no cancelling, no swapping. A boost lasts exactly as long as the plant's own harvest cycle — not a fixed day count, since cycle length itself changes with garden tier. Applying a boost is deliberately not offered on a fully-grown (ready) plant, since a finished plant has already stopped earning and the card would be burned for zero effect.

Early-pull outcome curve

The Farm Scene (Overhead Map)

This is what GREEN LIFE's 3D room becomes in Simple Farmer — and it is deliberately NOT 3D. No Three.js, no canvas, no image files for the world itself: one inline SVG drawn from plain shapes, scaled by viewBox to any screen size, computed live off real game state.

"this map would go where the 3d room is. just a visual with action abilities like harvest, plant, boost." — Cody's spec, quoted directly in the source

Three reasons it's deliberately not 3D

  1. GREEN LIFE's 3D room is the single heaviest thing in that codebase (draw calls, textures, shadow maps, a whole documented lag-fix history) — a picture that costs nothing to render keeps Simple Farmer the genuinely simple game.
  2. An SVG scales to any phone with no art pipeline and no asset loading — nothing to go missing, nothing to wait on.
  3. Every number on it is read live from game state, so the picture can never drift out of step with reality the way a pre-made image would.

What's real vs. what's pure decoration

The five garden patches, their plot dots, and the plants in them are REAL — sourced directly from live game state and updated as the player plays. The barn, farmhouse, dirt path, sun and clouds are pure decoration — no mechanics behind them at all, nothing to click, nothing to buy, nothing to upgrade. They exist purely so the screen reads as a farm and not a chart.

Each garden patch is drawn at a fixed layout position, keyed by tier id (not list order), so an unreachable/reordered tier is simply skipped rather than silently drawn on top of another one. Plots pack into a grid sized to fit the patch, with the last row centered rather than left-hugging. Toxic Area gets a distinct pulsing sickly glow and diagonal hazard-stripe border, since it is one plot at 100× power and should visually read as the dangerous corner of the farm.

A planted crop's emoji physically grows in size as it matures (from ~1.05× to ~1.6× the plot's own dot radius) — "the fastest way to read a whole field at a glance without putting a number on every plot." A ready-to-harvest plot gets a pulsing gold ring and switches its fill/border to gold, so finished plants are findable without reading anything.

Empty-state routing (4 distinct branches)

The scene picks its empty-state message and call-to-action based on exactly why the farm is empty — not a generic "plant something": no plots owned yet routes to the Garden; plots owned but a card in hand routes to the Garden; plots owned but everything is still sealed in a pack routes to My Stuff; a genuine fallback (nothing owned, nothing in hand) points at the pack carousel. This exists specifically because a brand-new player's first card is locked inside the sealed Starter Pack — sending them straight to an empty Garden with an empty inventory would be a dead end.

Money Flow & Burn Discipline

Two distinct $GREEN spend paths

FunctionReferral cut?Used by
burnGreenFull(amount, reason)Never — straight 100% burnCrossbreeding, farm-level upgrades
spendGreen(amount, reason)Yes — 4.01% carved off the top, remainder burned$GREEN-priced pack purchases

Cody's own words on the split, quoted directly in source: "cross breeds should 100% burn green no refer fee" and (for GREEN LIFE, mirrored here) "no refer cut on farm level upgrades for both games." The pack-purchase quote: "the green paid packs should take the refer cut off the top and then burn the rest."

SOL spend routing

spendSol(amount, reason) routes every SOL charge (or an auto-swapped USDC/USDT charge) through the same LP-phase table used at farm entry (§04/§10) — whatever the current total pool depth is decides the LP-vs-treasury split at the moment of the transaction.

Where the referral cut actually goes when it can't be paid

In this single-device simulation, a cut owed to a real (non-bootstrap) referrer has nobody live to pay — it accumulates in refChainPayout, an honestly-tracked running total, rather than being silently dropped or wrongly credited to the paying player's own referral-earnings pot (a different pot entirely — that one is what a player earns as a referrer, not what they pay as someone who was referred). Any portion of the cut that resolves all the way up to the bootstrap chain's burn seed is destroyed for real, immediately, regardless of backend status.

Burn sources, consolidated

  • $GREEN pack purchases (remainder after the referral cut)
  • Crossbreeding (100%, no cut)
  • Farm-level upgrades (100%, no cut)
  • Forfeited harvest pots (a plant that died or was penalized) — burned, never returned to the pool
  • The referral cut's own portion that reaches the bootstrap chain's burn seed

What is NOT burned

  • Any SOL-priced purchase — routed to the LP/treasury split instead
  • The lottery fee — 100% treasury, a deliberate exception (§15)
  • The marketplace fee's design-target 5% treasury share (the burn half of the marketplace fee is not currently executed in the simulated build — see §13)

Open Questions & Flagged Concerns

Issues the game's own source explicitly flags as unresolved, plus gaps in the research this report is honest about rather than papering over.

PRIMO's Legendary rate vs. this game's thin Legendary roster 25% Legendary on PRIMO was ported directly from GREEN LIFE for parity, but Simple Farmer has only two Legendary crops total. One crate a day at 25% mints Legendaries far faster than the base pack's 0.5% rate — explicitly flagged in the source as an easy dial to turn down, not yet turned.
The marketplace's own buyer sign-sheet vs. its fee panel disagree on the current split The buy-side wallet-sign screen states the entire 10% fee books to treasury today; the fee panel elsewhere on the same screen still shows the target 90/5/5 breakdown. Both are individually honest about what they're describing (one is "what happens right now," the other is "the real design"), but a player reading only one of the two could come away with a different understanding of where their money went on a purchase they just made.
No confirmed path for a player's own listing to actually sell The buy handler only operates against the NPC-seeded pool; no mechanism was found in the research for a simulated buyer to purchase a player's own myListings entry. As built, a player listing appears to always run its full 7 days and auto-return rather than genuinely sell — not confirmed as broken end-to-end, but not confirmed as working either, and not called out anywhere in the source as a known limitation.
SIM_FLAGS total-count drift See §25 — the list content is consistent (15 entries) everywhere it was captured, but the total-count figure quoted alongside it disagrees between research passes ("14," "15," and a rendered "16").
Stale documentation inside the game's own code At least two comments in farmer-constants.js were found to describe numbers that had already moved on: a PRIMO-pricing rationale comment still compares against a "$10 entry" figure after farm entry was raised to $25, and the buy-in-split comment in mintFarm() still literally says "$10 entry" in its prose even though every actual number it computes uses $25. Neither affects behavior — both are comment-only drift — but both are worth a cleanup pass.
